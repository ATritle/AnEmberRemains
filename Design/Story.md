# An Ember Remains

For nine nights, a dead woman has been speaking through the ashes of her own funeral pyre.

On the tenth, her sister answers.

## The heroine

**Mara Vey** is a red-haired spellcaster who earns her living removing curses, speaking to the recently dead, and performing the small, forbidden magics respectable people secretly need.

Her younger sister, **Elin**, was the only person who never feared her.

When a wasting sickness swept their town, the religious order known as the **Keepers of the Vigil** took the afflicted into their care. Elin was among them. Days later, the Keepers returned sealed urns and told the families their loved ones had died peacefully.

Mara tried to speak to her sister’s ashes.

Something answered.

It knew a childhood promise. It remembered a secret neither sister had ever shared. And beneath its voice, Mara could hear iron striking stone.

*“I’m still here. Below the bells.”*

That night, one ember appeared among the cold ashes. It has not gone out since.

## What lies beneath

The Keepers’ cathedral stands above **the Crucible**, an underground complex built by a vanished civilization.

Its upper levels contain burial chapels, abandoned workshops, and prison cells. Farther down lie enormous engines joined by black glass conduits, surgical chambers built for bodies unlike any living creature, and doors that open only when given a memory.

The Crucible was built to separate the soul from the body without destroying either.

Its creators hoped to abolish death.

Instead, they discovered that souls could be burned.

A single human life could power their wonders for years. Their civilization flourished around that discovery, then consumed itself trying to sustain it.

Centuries later, the Keepers found the machinery and put it back to work. The warmth beneath the city, its protective wards, and the apparent miracles of the cathedral all come from the same source.

The sick were never being treated. They were being delivered.

## Why Mara descends

Elin is still alive, but her soul has been threaded into the Crucible. The ember in Mara’s possession is the small piece that escaped when she called to her sister.

At the coming eclipse, the Keepers will perform **the Great Kindling**. Every captive soul will be consumed at once, awakening the intelligence imprisoned at the bottom of the complex.

Mara enters to free Elin before that happens.

The ember leads her toward her sister, but the Crucible recognizes it as stolen fuel. Once Mara passes its gates, its defenses awaken. Wardens hunt her. Sealed passages close. Things abandoned for centuries begin moving again.

She must destroy the guardians controlling each section to reach the chambers below.

## Her dark magic

Ordinary spells draw power from the living world. Inside the Crucible, that power grows weaker with every descent.

Mara survives through **cinderbinding**: forbidden magic that draws strength from the traces left behind when a soul burns.

She can turn grief into flame, bind an enemy with its own shadow, or unravel the enchantments keeping a ruined creature alive. Defeated enemies leave magical residue that she can shape into new spells.

But some residue still contains memories.

A new spell might bring a stranger’s last sight, a forgotten name, or the sensation of dying somewhere Mara has never been. As she grows stronger, the voices inside her become harder to distinguish from her own.

Elin’s voice remains the one she trusts.

## The descent

### 1. The Hushed Cloisters

The cathedral’s hidden foundations hold confiscated relics, failed healers, and pilgrims who discovered too much. The Keepers still operate here, capturing anyone who enters.

**Enemies:** masked wardens, bound penitents, animated ceremonial armor.

**Boss: Mother Caldris, Keeper of Mercy.**  
The woman who accepted Elin into the order has grafted healing instruments into her body. She can close almost any wound, including those Mara inflicts on her.

After defeating Caldris, Mara finds records proving that the supposed sickness was caused by the Crucible drawing life from the city above.

### 2. The Glassworks

Manufacturing halls produce vessels capable of containing souls. Their inhabitants have translucent armor, luminous veins, and bodies assembled around engraved metal frames.

**Enemies:** glass sentinels, discarded vessels, attendants wielding surgical magic.

**Boss: The Many-Handed Artisan.**  
An ancient craftsman suspended above his workshop, endlessly constructing a perfect body for a daughter whose soul was consumed centuries ago.

Here, Mara discovers that Elin has been designated a **living vessel**, reserved for something deeper in the Crucible.

### 3. The Garden of Ash

A buried sanctuary grows beneath an artificial moon. Pale trees bear fruit containing preserved memories. Roots wind through the bodies of those who came here hoping to forget.

**Enemies:** thorn-bound courtiers, predatory blooms, beasts grown around fragments of human consciousness.

**Boss: Lady Vesper, the Rootbound Queen.**  
One of the Crucible’s original rulers, Vesper has sustained herself by feeding on the memories of her subjects. She tempts Mara with a life in which Elin was never taken.

Mara learns that Elin has been resisting the machinery from within. Small acts of sabotage have opened routes and disabled traps throughout her descent.

### 4. The Choir Engine

Vast chambers hold thousands of suspended soul vessels. Their voices maintain the spells that keep the Crucible running. Silence is dangerous here: when a voice stops, something it was containing may escape.

**Enemies:** choir custodians, sound-warped constructs, escaped experiments.

**Boss: The First Cantor.**  
The founder of the Keepers has become a body of resonating metal and stolen voices. He speaks with the dead to make his enemies hesitate.

After his defeat, Mara finally hears Elin clearly.

Her sister explains that killing the intelligence below will destroy the system sustaining every captive—including her.

She has been guiding Mara down anyway.

### 5. The Seat of Kindling

At the bottom stands a throne inside an unfinished body large enough to fill a cathedral. Every conduit in the Crucible leads into it.

This is **Avarren, the Unending King**.

The ruler who commissioned the Crucible was also its first successful immortal. His consciousness survives by consuming other souls, but he can no longer leave the machinery.

The Great Kindling will give him a living body. Elin is its intended heart.

## The final boss

Avarren does not initially attack.

He offers Mara her sister, alive and whole. In return, Mara must take her place as his vessel. Her command of dark magic makes her far more valuable than Elin.

He admits that the Crucible causes suffering. He also shows Mara the wards it powers above: the city has grown dependent on the same machinery that is killing it.

Mara cannot save everyone by simply walking away.

The battle begins when she breaks the first conduit feeding his throne.

During the fight, Avarren moves between his immense mechanical body and a smaller, unfinished human form. As his defenses fail, he consumes the voices stored within the engine to restore himself.

Elin helps Mara sever those connections from inside.

To end him, Mara must finally extinguish the ember she has carried throughout the game.

The small piece of Elin that brought her this far becomes the spell that makes the immortal king mortal.

## The ending

Mara destroys Avarren and opens the soul vessels. The Crucible falls silent. Across the city above, the cathedral’s lights go out for the first time in centuries.

Elin has time for a few final words before she is gone.

Mara emerges at dawn carrying an empty urn.

In the days that follow, the city must learn to survive without its borrowed miracles. Mara helps where she can, though people still close their doors when they recognize the witch who extinguished the Vigil.

One evening, while lighting a hearth, she hears a familiar laugh in the crackle of the wood.

It may be a memory left by her magic.

She sits beside the fire a little longer.

**An ember remains.**
